Build a stronger Handshake profile

Keep Handshake open and use this only when you get stuck. You do not need to complete anything here before moving on.

The goal is not to fill every box. Focus on the parts that help Handshake show you better opportunities and help employers understand what you are interested in and what you can do.

What do you need help with?

Use “Looking for” to improve what Handshake shows you

These choices shape recommendations. They are not permanent commitments.

Best mindset: Choose what you would genuinely click on today. You can broaden or change it later.

Roles

Pick job titles that sound close enough to explore—not only your “dream job.”

Instead of: one exact title
Try: a few related roles that use similar skills.

Industries

Think about where the work could happen. The same role can exist in many industries.

A communications role could exist in higher education, healthcare, nonprofits, government, technology, and more.

Locations

Choose places you would realistically consider. Add more than one if you are flexible.

Opportunity type

Match this to what you want now: internship, full-time, part-time, campus employment, or networking/inspiration.

If you are still figuring things out: Broader is better than random. Choose a few directions you are curious about rather than selecting everything.

Make your summary useful in 2–3 lines

Handshake allows up to 300 characters. Think quick introduction, not mini cover letter.

Include

  • what you are studying, exploring, or seeking
  • the kind of work or field that interests you
  • one useful strength, skill, or experience

Skip

  • “hardworking, motivated, passionate” with no context
  • a long life story
  • copying your resume objective word-for-word

Need a starting point? Choose the situation closest to yours and replace the brackets.

0 / 300 characters
Quick test: Could someone reading this tell what you are interested in and one thing you bring? If yes, it is doing its job.

Choose skills you can back up

Handshake recommends 10 or fewer. More is not automatically better.

Fastest method: Look at your resume and one opportunity you would consider. Prioritize skills that appear in both.

Good choices

  • software and technical tools
  • research or data methods
  • languages
  • project management
  • writing or communication skills
  • customer service, leadership, teamwork, or problem solving when you have real examples

Leave it off if…

  • you selected it only because it sounds good
  • you could not give an example of using it
  • it is unrelated to anything you want to do
  • you already have several stronger, more specific skills
Better: Microsoft Excel, data analysis, social media strategy, Spanish, project management
Less useful: hardworking, friendly, responsible

Add enough experience to show what you have done

You do not need an internship before your profile has value.

Use your resume as your source. Do not re-brainstorm everything here. Add the experiences that best help someone understand your skills and interests.

Worth adding

  • jobs and internships
  • research or volunteer experience
  • leadership or meaningful organization involvement
  • projects that show relevant skills
  • courses only when they add useful context

You can skip

  • every organization you have ever joined
  • coursework that adds no new information
  • duplicate details that do not strengthen the profile
  • empty sections just to make the profile look “complete”
No directly related experience yet? Add work, campus involvement, volunteering, caregiving, research, or projects that demonstrate transferable skills. Employers still need evidence of how you work.

Choose a visibility setting intentionally

You can change this later.

Community

Employers, students, and alumni can view your profile. This supports the broadest networking and virtual event features.

Employers

Approved employers can view your profile, but students and alumni cannot.

Private

Your profile is not visible to employers or peers. You can still browse opportunities and use many Handshake features.

If employer discovery matters

Choose Community or Employers. Use the one that matches your comfort level.

Where to change it: In Handshake, open your account settings and review Privacy.

30-second profile check

Do this in Handshake—not here.

Interest: Can someone quickly tell what kinds of opportunities or fields you are exploring?
Evidence: Is there at least one experience, project, job, activity, or skill that shows what you can do?
Accuracy: Are your major, graduation date, locations, and other details current?
Visibility: Is your privacy setting what you intended?
Resume: If you plan to apply through Handshake, is your current resume uploaded under Documents?
If those are covered, stop editing. Use Handshake to search, save opportunities, attend events, and apply. Update your profile when your experience or goals change.
Handshake labels and features may change over time. This guide is designed to help you make decisions inside Handshake rather than recreate your profile somewhere else.
